# Stations: technique, mistakes, logging, progression

General coaching cues. For movement standards and weights, always defer to the event's current rulebook.
No-reps and penalties cost time, so tell the athlete to read the standards for every station.

Race order: SkiErg, sled push, sled pull, burpee broad jumps, row, farmers carry, sandbag lunges, wall balls.
Canonical names for logging: `skierg`, `sled_push`, `sled_pull`, `burpee_broad_jumps`, `row`,
`farmers_carry`, `sandbag_lunges`, `wall_balls`.

| Station | Technique cues | Common mistakes | Log |
|---|---|---|---|
| **SkiErg** (1000 m) | Hinge at hips, pull with lats and core, arms follow. Steady rhythm | Going out too hard in the first 200 m; all arms, no legs | time_s, avg pace if known, rpe |
| **Sled push** | Low body angle, arms locked, short fast steps, drive through the floor | Standing up too tall; long slow steps; stopping and restarting | weight_kg, distance_m, time_s, rpe |
| **Sled pull** | Sit back, hand-over-hand pulling, stay low, steady rope rhythm | Pulling with arms only; losing tension on the rope | weight_kg, time_s, rpe |
| **Burpee broad jumps** | Even rhythm, efficient chest-down and get-up, land and go. Pace for 80 m, not for the first 20 | Starting fast and fading; wasteful movement; skipping standards | reps or distance_m, time_s, rpe |
| **Row** (1000 m) | Drive with legs, then body, then arms. Steady pace slightly below max | Over-pulling early; shortening the stroke when tired | time_s, avg split if known, rpe |
| **Farmers carry** | Tall posture, tight grip, short quick steps. Grip is the limit | Letting grip go before the end; drifting | weight_kg, distance_m, time_s, unbroken |
| **Sandbag lunges** | Upright torso, bag stable on shoulders, steady rhythm, full range | Torso collapsing forward; rushing the first half | weight_kg, distance_m, time_s, rpe |
| **Wall balls** | Squat depth, drive through, hit the target height every rep. Plan sets, breathe on the way down | Going unbroken until failure; missing the target and getting no-reps | reps, sets, unbroken, weight_kg, time_s |

## What to log after a class
Weights and distances actually used, sets and breaks (unbroken or not), time per effort where known, and
RPE 1–10. Capture "felt heavy", "grip failed", "no-reps". They explain the data later.

## Finding the weakest station without race data
Use logs from the last 4–6 weeks:
- Which stations have the highest RPE at modest weights?
- After which station is the next run pace worst (compromised running)?
- Which stations were avoided or never logged?
Say plainly when you're guessing. Suggest a single station benchmark day (each station once at race
weights, rest between) to fill gaps.

## Progression
- Raise weight, distance, or reduce rest, but not all at once. One variable per week.
- Practice at race weights for sleds and carries at least a few times before race week.
- Build grip separately if carries or sled pulls fail on grip.
- Wall balls: build tolerance by accumulating reps in controlled sets, not by grinding to failure.
- Burpee broad jumps: practice the rhythm and the get-up, since fatigue shows here first.

## Technique over load
A first-timer with poor technique gains more from a lighter, clean rep than from the full race weight.
Recommend a coach check on sled, lunges and burpee mechanics, ideally in the athlete's lessons.
