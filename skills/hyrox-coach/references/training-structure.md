# Training structure around the running plan

Rules of thumb from common coaching practice, not validated formulas. Adapt to the athlete, and when in
doubt, be more conservative.

## Weekly layout
Garmin Coach (or another running plan) typically schedules about 4 runs a week: a Long Run, a Threshold
or VO2 Max session, a Base run. Build the rest around them:
- **Two quality sessions are the most a week should hold alongside the key runs.** Everything beyond
  that should be easy. Count a hard class as a quality session.
- **Leg-heavy station work (sleds, lunges, wall balls, burpee broad jumps) within 24 h before a key run
  is a clash.** Move it, lighten it, or swap with an upper-body or SkiErg/row day.
- **Best slots for station work:** the day after a key run, or the same day as an easy run (stations
  after the run). Keep at least one full rest or very easy day per week.
- **Strength:** two sessions a week while building, one in the peak and taper phases. Include
  lunges, carries, pulling and pushing patterns that match the stations.
- **Classes count.** A mixed class can be a station session, a conditioning session, or both. Ask what it
  was before deciding what the next day looks like.

## The key Hyrox-specific session: compromised running
Run hard-ish right after a station, because that is what the race does. Examples:
- Sled push or wall balls, then a 1 km run at planned race pace, repeated 3–5 times with short rest.
- Lunges, then 1 km at steady pace.
Do one a week while building. Start at steady effort, not all-out. The aim is learning to find your pace
with heavy legs, not setting a time. Log the run pace after each station: it shows which station costs
the most.

## Simulations
- **Half simulation** (4 runs + 4 stations, or all 8 stations with shorter runs): a good checkpoint
  3–4 weeks out. Record with a lap press at every run/station change so the data is usable.
- **Full simulation:** optional, once at most, only when the athlete is well conditioned, at least
  3 weeks before the race. A first-timer can skip it if the half simulation went well.
- Nothing hard in the final 7–10 days.
- After a simulation: expect 2–3 easy days. Check readiness before deciding.

## Phases by days to race
| Days to race | Focus |
|---|---|
| 8+ weeks | Build base and strength. Learn every station's technique. Establish baselines (1 km pace, station times at race weights) |
| 4–8 weeks | Build specificity: weekly compromised run, station work at or near race weights, half simulation |
| 2–4 weeks (peak) | Highest specificity, steady load. Final simulation, then stop adding volume |
| Final 7–10 days (taper) | Cut volume by roughly a third to a half, keep short sharp touches. Sleep, fuel, logistics |
| Race week | See `race-strategy.md` |

If the athlete has fewer than 6 weeks left and is a first-timer, skip building new fitness and focus on
consistency, technique, pacing and not getting injured.

## Load and recovery guardrails
- Raise weekly volume gradually; avoid big jumps. If the Garmin acute:chronic ratio or running impact
  vs tolerance is high, hold the load steady.
- Deload week (about 30% less) every 3–4 weeks while building, unless the taper is close.
- Sleep under 7 hours for several nights, or resting HR up 5+ bpm for several days, is a cue to
  reduce intensity, not just volume.
- Illness: easy walking only until symptoms are gone; no hard efforts with fever.

## Heat, floors and venues
- Indoor race halls are warm. Practice a hot or well-dressed session before race week if possible.
- Sled push weight feels very different between floors. If the athlete's gym sled runs lighter or
  heavier than the race surface, tell them not to judge their readiness from one gym session.
